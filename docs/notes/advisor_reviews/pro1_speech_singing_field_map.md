Here’s the 2024–2026 map I’d use as a researcher, as of **June 8, 2026**. I’m treating the field broadly: **singing voice conversion, singing style/technique conversion, speech↔singing conversion, cross-lingual timbre/speaker disentanglement, and SSL/audio-codec factorization**.

## Current zeitgeist

The frontier has shifted from **“make singer A sound like singer B”** to **“change how the same singer performs while preserving lyrics, melody, and identity.”** SVCC 2025 explicitly reframed the benchmark from singer-identity SVC to **singing style conversion**, with two tasks: in-domain style conversion and zero-shot style conversion where the source singer is unseen and the style reference comes from another singer. The challenge uses seven style/technique labels: breathy, falsetto, mixed voice, pharyngeal, glissando, vibrato, and control. ([Singing Voice Conversion Challenge](https://vc-challenge.org/ "Singing Voice Conversion Challenge 2025"))

The dominant system recipe is now **tokenize → autoregressive content/style/prosody modeling → diffusion or flow-matching acoustic generation → neural vocoder**. Vevo/Vevo1.5 and Vevo2 are the clearest examples: pretrained prosody and content-style tokenizers, AR token prediction, flow-matching mel generation, and Vocos-style vocoding; Vevo2 extends this into a unified speech/singing framework supporting TTS, text-to-singing, SVS, style-preserved and style-converted VC/SVC, editing, SSC, humming-to-singing, and instrument-to-singing. ([GitHub](https://github.com/open-mmlab/Amphion/tree/main/models/svc/vevosing "Amphion/models/svc/vevosing at main · open-mmlab/Amphion · GitHub"))

People are excited about **fine-grained technique control**, **zero-shot style transfer**, **preference tuning / DPO for style fidelity**, **unified speech–singing models**, and **representation factorization**. SVCC 2025 found that ARLM+diffusion/flow systems performed strongly, and the top style-similarity system fine-tuned Vevo1.5’s ARLM with LoRA/DPO; S2Voice, a 2026 SVCC-winning system description, explicitly adds FiLM-style conditioning and style-aware cross-attention to an AR LLM, plus speaker conditioning in the flow-matching stage and SFT/DPO training. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15629v1 "The Singing Voice Conversion Challenge 2025: From Singer Identity Conversion To Singing Style Conversion"))

The representation side is converging on **factorized SSL/codecs**: FACodec/NaturalSpeech3 factorizes content, prosody, timbre, and acoustic detail; LSCodec targets low-bitrate speaker-decoupled tokens; WavTokenizer tries to make compact acoustic codec tokens semantically rich enough for audio language models. ([Proceedings of Machine Learning Research](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ju24b.html "NaturalSpeech 3: Zero-Shot Speech Synthesis with Factorized Codec and Diffusion Models"))

## Claims that are now saturated or weakly novel

“Zero-shot SVC from a short reference clip” is no longer enough. SeedVC already offers zero-shot VC and SVC with released models, and Vevo/Vevo2 provide larger unified pipelines with checkpoints. A new paper needs a harder claim: style/technique control, cross-lingual generalization, leakage reduction, objective evaluation, low-resource training, or a clearly better open benchmark result. ([GitHub](https://github.com/Plachtaa/seed-vc "GitHub - Plachtaa/seed-vc: zero-shot voice conversion & singing voice conversion, with real-time support · GitHub"))

“HuBERT/ContentVec/Whisper content encoder + F0 + diffusion/vocoder” is mostly a baseline pattern now, not a thesis by itself. The novelty has moved to **what information leaks through those representations**, how style and timbre are separated, and whether the model controls dynamic techniques such as vibrato, glissando, and breathiness. SVCC 2025’s result paper explicitly identifies these dynamic styles as the hardest cases. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15629v1 "The Singing Voice Conversion Challenge 2025: From Singer Identity Conversion To Singing Style Conversion"))

“High naturalness MOS on demos” is also a weak claim unless paired with a common benchmark, listening-test design, style-similarity evaluation, and leakage analysis. SVCC 2025 evaluated 26 systems and found that no system achieved human-level naturalness; objective metrics such as neural MOS and singer/speaker embeddings had useful correlations, but the organizers still concluded that objective metrics need more work. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15629v1 "The Singing Voice Conversion Challenge 2025: From Singer Identity Conversion To Singing Style Conversion"))

“Scaling data fixes it” is not a complete story. Vevo1.5/2 benefit from very large speech data plus internal SingNet singing data, while open researchers often have only hundreds of hours or less of clean singing data. That creates a real opening for **data-efficient adapters, representation probes, and low-resource controlled SVC**. ([GitHub](https://github.com/open-mmlab/Amphion/tree/main/models/svc/vevosing "Amphion/models/svc/vevosing at main · open-mmlab/Amphion · GitHub"))

## Clearly unsolved problems

The most visible unsolved problem is **dynamic singing-style modeling**. SVCC 2025 reports that breathy, glissando, and vibrato were the hardest styles, with style accuracy around 37.3%, 42.6%, and 43.9%, respectively; the paper attributes this to time-varying noise, rapid F0 modulation, and continuous F0 drift. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15629v1 "The Singing Voice Conversion Challenge 2025: From Singer Identity Conversion To Singing Style Conversion"))

A second unsolved problem is **true disentanglement**: lyrics/content, melody/F0, singer timbre, vocal technique, language, accent, and recording/acoustic detail are still entangled. LASPA frames this clearly for multilingual speaker embeddings, where linguistic information leaks into speaker representations; FreeSVC and EZ-VC are adjacent attempts to make cross-lingual or multilingual conversion work better, but the general singing case remains open. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02083 "[2506.02083] LASPA: Language Agnostic Speaker Disentanglement with Prefix-Tuned Cross-Attention"))

Speech↔singing conversion remains immature compared with SVC. SVPT says STS is bottlenecked by paired-data scarcity, content–pitch alignment, and output quality; singing-to-speech work frames S2S as a new cross-domain conversion task that must reduce pitch, rhythm, and timbre variation while preserving phonetic information. ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.585/ "Self-Supervised Singing Voice Pre-Training towards Speech-to-Singing Conversion - ACL Anthology"))

Evaluation is still a publishable problem. SVCC 2025 found useful correlations for neural MOS and singer/speaker embeddings, but concluded objective metrics still need improvement; this is especially true for **technique similarity**, where signal-level descriptors of vibrato, breathiness, spectral tilt, slide rate, and F0 modulation are not yet standardized. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15629v1 "The Singing Voice Conversion Challenge 2025: From Singer Identity Conversion To Singing Style Conversion"))

## Central groups, labs, and challenges

The central benchmark node is the **VCC/SVCC challenge series**. SVCC 2025 is organized around Nagoya/Toda Lab, CUHK-Shenzhen/Zhizheng Wu’s group, CMU/Jiatong Shi, and NII/Yusuke Yasuda, and it provides the most important common evaluation setting for this topic. ([Singing Voice Conversion Challenge](https://vc-challenge.org/ "Singing Voice Conversion Challenge 2025"))

The strongest open-system ecosystem is around **Amphion / Vevo / Vevo2**, associated with Zhizheng Wu and collaborators, because it provides code, checkpoints, and recipes for speech, singing, and style-converted generation. ([GitHub](https://github.com/open-mmlab/Amphion/blob/main/models/svc/vevo2/README.md "Amphion/models/svc/vevo2/README.md at main · open-mmlab/Amphion · GitHub"))

For datasets and singing-specific modeling, **Zhejiang University / Zhou Zhao’s group** is central through GTSinger and SVPT; GTSinger is especially important because SVCC 2025 uses a GTSinger subset and because it provides technique labels and benchmark code. ([GitHub](https://github.com/AaronZ345/GTSinger "GitHub - AaronZ345/GTSinger: Dataset and code of GTSinger(NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight): A Global Multi-Technique Singing Corpus with Realistic Music Scores for All Singing Tasks · GitHub"))

For representation factorization, the key nodes are **Microsoft Research / NaturalSpeech3-FACodec**, **X-LANCE / LSCodec**, and the **WavTokenizer** line from Zhejiang/Alibaba/Meta collaborators. ([Proceedings of Machine Learning Research](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ju24b.html "NaturalSpeech 3: Zero-Shot Speech Synthesis with Factorized Codec and Diffusion Models"))

## Code/checkpoint reality check

Most immediately usable: **SeedVC**, **Vevo1.5/Vevo2 via Amphion**, **Serenade**, **HQ-SVC**, **GTSinger**, **FreeSVC**, **FACodec**, **LSCodec**, **WavTokenizer**, and **singing-to-speech generative flow**. The caveat is compute: Vevo-style systems are usable for inference and adaptation, but training full AR+flow+vocoder stacks is not a small-GPU project. ([GitHub](https://github.com/open-mmlab/Amphion/tree/main/models/svc/vevosing "Amphion/models/svc/vevosing at main · open-mmlab/Amphion · GitHub"))

Likely paper-only or demo-first from the sources I checked: **S2Voice**, **SVPT**, **LASPA**, and **SSANSVC**. They are still worth reading because they define the frontier, but I would not build a master’s project that depends on reproducing them from scratch.

## Best small-GPU master’s directions

1. **Dynamic-style adapter for vibrato/glissando/breathy conversion.** Use Serenade, SeedVC, HQ-SVC, or Vevo2 inference as the base, freeze most of the model, and train small LoRA/adapters or explicit style-control heads on GTSinger/SVCC-style labels. The target contribution is not “better SVC,” but “better dynamic technique control with measurable leakage reduction.”
    
2. **Representation leakage audit for singing.** Extract HuBERT/ContentVec/Whisper, FACodec, LSCodec, WavTokenizer, and maybe Vevo content-style tokens on the same singing clips. Train lightweight probes for singer ID, language, F0, style label, lyrics/phoneme content, and recording condition. This is cheap, publishable, and directly addresses the field’s disentanglement uncertainty.
    
3. **Metric suite for singing-style conversion.** Build an evaluation toolkit for vibrato depth/rate, glissando slope, breathiness/noise ratio, spectral tilt, F0 stability, singer embedding similarity, and style-classifier agreement. SVCC 2025 explicitly says objective metrics need improvement, so a rigorous open metric package could matter even without a new generator.
    
4. **Speech-to-singing alignment with lightweight token resampling.** Start from SVPT’s problem statement, but avoid training a large model. Use pretrained tokens plus CTC/DTW/duration modeling to map speech rhythm to singing rhythm, then synthesize through an existing vocoder or SVC backend. Focus on alignment and intelligibility rather than end-to-end generation.
    
5. **Cross-lingual singing timbre disentanglement.** Use FreeSVC/multilingual singing data and LASPA-style language-adversarial or prefix-tuned speaker embeddings. A tight thesis could ask: “Which representations preserve singer timbre under unseen-language vowel inventories without leaking lyrics/language?”
    

My strongest recommendation: **do direction 1 or 2**. Direction 1 is more generative and demo-friendly; direction 2 is lower compute, more scientific, and easier to make rigorous.
